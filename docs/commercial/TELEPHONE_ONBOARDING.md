# Using ApexVoice With Your Existing Phone Number

A guide for Harbour and Main ApexVoice customers.
Last updated 2026-11-28. Carrier steps in this guide were checked against each carrier published support pages on that date. Carriers change their pages and plans, so if a step does not match what you see, stop and contact us before guessing.

## The short version

You do not have to give up your phone number to use ApexVoice.

Most businesses keep their number exactly where it is and tell their phone company to send some calls to a new ApexVoice number we provide. Your staff can still answer first. ApexVoice picks up the calls nobody gets to, the calls that arrive while you are on another call, and, on phone systems that support schedules, the calls that arrive after you close.

There are four ways to connect. They are called Path A, B, C, and D in this guide.

- Path A: Get a new dedicated ApexVoice number. Your current number does not change at all.
- Path B: Forward calls from your current number to your ApexVoice number. This is what most businesses choose. It has four settings: all calls, unanswered calls, busy calls, and after-hours calls.
- Path C: Move (port) your number to ApexVoice. Your number becomes an ApexVoice number and your old phone service for that number ends.
- Path D: Human transfer. This is not a setup path on its own. It is how ApexVoice hands a caller to a real person at your business, and it works with A, B, and C.

## Before you start: an important promise and a warning

Harbour and Main does not change your carrier settings and does not port your number without your written approval and your own carrier account access. You stay in control of your carrier account at all times. We will tell you exactly what to dial or click, or we will walk through it with you while you are signed in to your own account. We never need your carrier password.

Warning: Never port a number before confirming what else runs on that line. A single business line can also carry your internet, your alarm system, a fax machine, or a payment terminal. Porting the number ends the old service for that line and can break those things, including alarm monitoring. Path B forwarding does not have this problem, which is one reason we recommend it first.

Two more things to know before you choose:

- Forwarding does not move your voicemail. If a call forwards to ApexVoice, your old voicemail does not take that message. ApexVoice takes the message instead, using your ApexVoice settings. Calls that do not forward still use your old voicemail as before, unless your carrier says otherwise below.
- Your existing voicemail greeting still matters. On some carriers, voicemail and forwarding fight over the same calls. Rogers mobile is the clearest example: conditional forwarding does not work while Rogers voicemail is active. Where that applies, this guide says so in the carrier steps.

## Path A: Dedicated ApexVoice number

When to choose it:

- You want zero risk to your current number while you try ApexVoice.
- You are opening a new location or adding a new published number.
- You want a separate after-hours or overflow number to print on your website, vehicles, or Google listing.
- Your current carrier cannot forward the way you need (this happens with some TELUS landlines, see Path B).

How it works:

- We set up a new Canadian local number for you on ApexVoice. The published price for a local number is $0.50 USD per month, and it is included in your ApexVoice service arrangement with us rather than billed to you by the carrier.
- You publish the new number where you want ApexVoice to answer, or you forward to it later using Path B when you are ready.
- Your current number, carrier, and voicemail do not change.

What to test after setup:

- Call the new ApexVoice number from a mobile phone. Confirm the greeting names your business correctly.
- Ask a question only your business can answer, such as your hours, and confirm the answer matches your approved information.
- Leave a test message and confirm it reaches the right person by the delivery method you chose.
- If you plan to transfer to a human, ask for a person and confirm the transfer reaches your transfer target.

How to turn it off:

- Stop publishing the number, or remove any forwarding that points to it (see Path B rollback steps). Tell us and we will release the number. Nothing about your original number is affected.

## Path B: Forward your existing number to ApexVoice

When to choose it:

- You want customers to keep calling the number they already know.
- You want to keep your carrier, your contract, and everything else on your line.
- You want to be able to undo the change in under a minute. Forwarding can always be turned off.

The four forwarding settings:

- All calls (unconditional): Every call goes straight to ApexVoice. Your phone does not ring. Best for full-time answering, holidays, and closures.
- Unanswered calls (no answer): Your phone rings first. If nobody picks up after a set number of rings, the call goes to ApexVoice. This is the setting most businesses want during the day.
- Busy: Calls go to ApexVoice only while your line is in use. Best if you have one line and hate giving customers a busy signal.
- After hours: On phone systems with schedules, calls go to ApexVoice outside your business hours and ring normally during them. On traditional landlines there is no clock. You would dial the all-calls code when you close and the off code when you open, every day. Most landline customers skip the daily ritual and use unanswered plus busy forwarding all day instead, which covers most missed calls with no daily work.

Our default recommendation: unanswered plus busy, on all day. Staff answer first, ApexVoice catches the rest, and nobody has to remember codes at closing time.

### Bell business landline (Local Link)

These steps are from Bell published business support instructions.

- All calls: Lift the receiver, press *72 and then your ApexVoice number. Listen for two beeps, then hang up. To cancel, press *73 and listen for two beeps.
- Busy: Press *90. When prompted, key in your ApexVoice number. Listen for two beeps, then hang up. To cancel, press *91. Note: Bell says this feature is only available on Local Link without voicemail or with optional programmable voicemail. If your line has Bell programmed voicemail, ask Bell to change it before your setup session.
- Unanswered: Press *92 and then your ApexVoice number. Listen for two beeps, then hang up. Calls forward if not answered after 4 rings. To cancel, press *93.
- After hours: No schedule on this service. Use all calls at closing (*72) and cancel each morning (*73), or use unanswered plus busy all day.

- Busy: Dial *90, then enter your ApexVoice number. Also available on the desk phone as Call Forward Busy.

### Rogers business landline, Business Phone (Rogers together with Shaw)

- All calls, from the phone: Lift the receiver, dial *72, wait for the dial tone, enter your ApexVoice number (add a 1 in front if it is long distance; long-distance charges apply). The ApexVoice line will ring; since ApexVoice answers, stay on the line briefly, then hang up. If it does not connect, hang up and repeat the steps once. To turn off, dial *73 and hang up when you hear the stutter tone.
- All calls, online: Sign in to your self-serve portal, scroll to the phone section, click Manage beside Call Forwarding, turn Forward incoming calls on, enter your ApexVoice number with the area code, and Save.
- Unanswered and busy: A Rogers Business representative has to turn these features on for your line before you can use them. Call Rogers Business support before your ApexVoice setup session and ask them to enable Call Forward Busy and Call Forward No Answer. Once enabled, from the phone at your business: dial *92 and your ApexVoice number for unanswered calls, or *90 and your ApexVoice number for busy calls. Hang up when you hear the stutter tone. Turn off with *93 (unanswered) or *91 (busy). These two features can only be managed from the phone at your business, not online.
- Notes: Call Forward Busy cannot be used if your number is part of a multi-line hunt. Call Waiting does not work while all-calls forwarding is on. Calls that go unanswered at the ApexVoice end will not come back to your Rogers voicemail.

### Rogers IBLc business phone

To turn off, dial *73, listen for two beeps, and hang up. If you subscribe to voicemail and forward to another number, your voicemail will not take messages for forwarded calls.
- Busy (Enhanced Call Forward): Dial *90, listen for the confirmation tone, enter your 10-digit ApexVoice number, and stay on the line at least five seconds if it answers. If busy or no answer, dial *90 again and redial the number; two beeps confirm. Turn off with *91.
- Unanswered (Enhanced Call Forward): Dial *92, listen for the confirmation tone, enter your 10-digit ApexVoice number, stay at least five seconds if it answers, then hang up. If busy or no answer, repeat for two beeps. Turn off with *93.
- Important conflict: If you subscribe to Rogers Business Voicemail, you cannot subscribe to Enhanced Call Forward, because unanswered calls go to voicemail. Choose one. If you want ApexVoice to take unanswered calls, we will help you plan the voicemail change with Rogers before your setup session.

### What to test after setup: the test call checklist

Do these with us on another line, right after setup, and again after any carrier change. Do not skip the busy test; it is the one most often missed.

- During hours test: Call your business number from a mobile. Let it ring. Confirm your phone rings first (for unanswered and busy setups), then ApexVoice answers with the right business name and greeting.
- Let it ring through test: Call again and do not answer. Count the rings before ApexVoice picks up and confirm that timing feels right for your customers. Adjust ring settings if your system allows it and test again.
- Busy test: Make your business line busy by calling it and staying on the line, or by taking the phone off the hook where that is safe. From a third phone, call the business number. Confirm ApexVoice answers instead of a busy signal, if you set up busy forwarding.
- After-hours test: If you use a schedule, test just after closing time, or temporarily set the schedule to make it after hours now, then call and confirm ApexVoice answers right away. Put the schedule back and confirm the confirmation.
- Message test: Leave a message as a test caller with a name and callback number. Confirm the message reaches the right inbox or phone, with the correct name and number.
- Transfer test: Call, ask for a person, and confirm the call reaches your transfer target and that the person can hear the caller clearly. Confirm what number shows on the receiving phone so your team learns to recognize transferred calls.
- Voicemail test: After forwarding is on, confirm you understand where messages now land. Forwarded calls should leave messages with ApexVoice, not your old voicemail. Call your old voicemail and confirm no test message landed there by mistake.
- Rollback test: Turn forwarding off using the steps for your carrier, call your number, and confirm your phone rings normally again. Then turn forwarding back on and confirm ApexVoice answers again. Knowing rollback works removes the fear of trying this.

### How to turn it off or roll back

Forwarding is always reversible, and you do not need us to do it.

- Bell landline: *73 for all calls, *91 for busy, *93 for unanswered.
- Bell Total Connect: Turn the forwarding option off on the desk phone or in the portal, or dial the same family of codes in reverse as shown by Bell for your service during setup. We will write your exact off steps on your setup summary.
- Rogers Business Phone (Shaw): *73 for all calls, *91 for busy, *93 for unanswered, from the phone at your business.
- Rogers IBLc: *73 for all calls, *91 for busy, *93 for unanswered. You can also use Business Online Manager or Remote Access.
- Rogers Advantage Voice: MyBusiness Hub, Rogers Unison, User Call Settings, toggle Call Forward off, Save.
- Rogers mobile: ##21# cancels all-calls, ##67# cancels busy, ##61# cancels unanswered, ##62# cancels not reachable. A single # instead of ## pauses most of these instead of cancelling; use ## to fully remove.
- TELUS: Use the off step we confirm for your line during setup and record on your setup summary. For TELUS mobile, turn the forwarding type off in Call Settings.
- RingCentral: Settings, Phone, Call handling, turn off Forward all, or change Missed calls back to voicemail. Save.
- Vonage: Enterprise Portal, Voice Settings, Call Forwarding, turn the options off.
- Ooma Office: Dial *74 to toggle, or change the call handling preference back to Ring my phone or voicemail.
- VoIP.ms: Manage DID, change Routing back to your previous setting, apply changes.
- Fongo: Follow Me Settings, set Status to Disable, Submit. In the app, set the forwarding mode to Never.
- Path A: Just stop using the ApexVoice number. Your original service was never changed.
- Path C: Porting back is a new port to your chosen carrier and takes time. This is why we treat porting as a considered decision, not a trial.

If anything behaves strangely, turn forwarding off first and call us second. Your customers reaching your normal phone or voicemail again is the safe state.

## Path C: Move your number to ApexVoice (porting)

When to choose it:

- You are leaving your current phone provider anyway.
- You want ApexVoice to be your phone service for that number, with no forwarding in the middle.
- Forwarding cannot do what you need on your current service, and you accept a permanent move after a careful audit.

When not to choose it:

- The same line carries internet, an alarm, a fax machine, or a card terminal, until each of those has a new home.
- You are still in a contract you have not priced out.
- You only want to try ApexVoice. Use Path A or B for a trial. Porting is not a trial.

How porting works in Canada, in plain terms:

- You have the right to keep your number when you change phone companies, as long as you stay in the same local exchange area (roughly, the same community your number belongs to). This is a CRTC rule, not a favour from your carrier.
- You ask the new provider to move the number. You do not cancel with the old provider first. In fact, you must not cancel first: only a number that is still active can be moved. The move itself ends the old service for that number.
- You, or someone you authorize, must approve the move. For ApexVoice, that means a signed Letter of Authorization. SignalWire requires it to be signed and dated within the past 30 days before the port can start.
- Moving between landlines, between mobile phones, and between landline and mobile is all supported in most of Canada, within the same exchange.

What we will need from you:

- The number or numbers to move, exactly as they appear on your bill.
- Your current provider name and your account number with them.
- Whether the account is business or residential.
- Your company name and the name of the person authorized on the account, exactly as the carrier has them.
- The billing phone number and the phone service address, exactly as the carrier has them.
- Any PIN on the account.
- A recent bill or carrier service record if we need it to match their records. Small differences, such as a missing Inc. in the company name or an old service address, are the most common reason a port is rejected.

Cost and timing:

- Porting a number into SignalWire costs nothing. SignalWire publishes port-in as free for local and toll-free numbers. If you later port away from SignalWire, SignalWire charges $5.00 USD per number to port out.
- Small port requests usually complete within 7 business days when the information is correct. Canadian numbers can take up to two weeks. We will not promise a faster date, and we will schedule your move so your number is configured and tested in ApexVoice before it switches.
- Your old carrier may have contract terms or final charges. Those are between you and them, and we will ask you to confirm them before we submit anything.

What can delay or reject a port:

- A name, address, account number, or PIN that does not match the carrier record.
- A number that is not active, or a number included by mistake.
- Several requests with conflicting information at the same time.
- If a port is rejected, SignalWire tells us by email and the request waits for corrected information. If nobody responds within a week, SignalWire cancels the request and closes the case, so we will stay on top of it with you.

Warnings specific to porting:

- DSL internet on the same line will stop when the line service ends. Arrange new internet before the port date if your internet rides on your phone line.
- Alarm systems that dial out on the line will stop reporting. Talk to your alarm company first. This is a safety issue, not a paperwork issue.
- Fax machines and dial-up card terminals on the line will stop working.
- If this number anchors a bundle, the bundle and its discounts can change or end.
- 911: Emergency address registrations do not move with a port. We will register your service address for the number in ApexVoice as part of the move, and you should confirm it on your setup summary.
- Text messaging: If customers text this number, tell us before the port. Messaging setup is separate from voice setup.

How a port rolls back:

- Before the switch date, we can withdraw the request.
- After the switch, going back means a new port to the carrier you choose, with the same information matching and timing as any port. There is no undo button. We will make sure you understand this before you sign.

## Path D: Human transfer

When ApexVoice has a caller who needs a person, it can transfer the call live to a number you choose.

- No setup is needed at your carrier. Your number is simply the number ApexVoice calls. Forwarding is about calls coming to ApexVoice. Transfer is about ApexVoice calling you. They are separate.
- How it works: The caller is talking to ApexVoice. ApexVoice places a second call to your transfer target and connects the two. You answer, and you are speaking to the caller.
- Pick a good transfer target: A number a person answers during the hours we promise a handoff. A direct staff mobile or a desk line that does not forward back to ApexVoice is best.
- Avoid the loop: If your main number forwards unanswered calls to ApexVoice, and ApexVoice transfers callers to that same main number, a transfer that nobody picks up can bounce back to ApexVoice. Sometimes that is fine, because ApexVoice can then take a message. Sometimes it confuses callers. We will test your exact setup and agree on the behaviour with you.
- Cost note: A transferred call uses an outbound call from ApexVoice for as long as you and the caller talk, and transfers are part of your ApexVoice usage. Nothing is charged to your carrier account for receiving the transfer beyond your normal plan treatment for incoming calls.

## If you are not sure which path fits

- Keep your number and your carrier, and want staff to answer first: Path B, unanswered plus busy.
- Want ApexVoice to answer everything, starting now, with an easy undo: Path B, all calls.
- Have a cloud phone system (RingCentral, TELUS Business Connect, Rogers Advantage Voice, Vonage, Ooma) and fixed hours: Path B with a schedule for after hours, plus unanswered and busy during the day.

## What Harbour and Main does and does not do

- We prepare your ApexVoice number, your greeting, your business information, and your message and transfer rules.
- We give you the exact steps for your carrier, or we join you by phone while you enter them in your own account or on your own phone.
- We run the full test call checklist with you and write down your setup, including your exact off steps, on a setup summary for your files.
- We do not sign in to your carrier account for you, we do not ask for your carrier password, and we do not submit a port without your signed authorization and written approval.
- We do not charge your carrier account. Any carrier charges for forwarding features or minutes are billed by your carrier under your plan, and this guide flags the ones we know about so there are no surprises.
