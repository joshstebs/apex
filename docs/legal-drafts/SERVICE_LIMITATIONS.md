# ApexVoice Service Limitations, Draft

Provider: Harbour & Main, operating ApexVoice
Applies to: Business customers, their staff, and callers, and to all sales, onboarding, and support material for ApexVoice
Date of draft: November 28, 2026

## How to use this document

These limitations are part of the service, not fine print to be hidden. They must be reflected in sales pages, demonstrations, onboarding, templates, and support answers. If marketing wording conflicts with this document, this document is the accurate statement and the marketing wording must be corrected.

## Not for emergency calls

- ApexVoice is not an emergency service and must never be presented as one.
- In an emergency, callers must call 911, or the appropriate local emergency number outside Canada.
- The receptionist should be configured, where the system supports it, to respond to a caller describing an emergency by telling them to call 911 immediately, and by following the business escalation rule if one applies. The receptionist cannot dispatch help, cannot contact emergency services on the caller behalf unless a specific, tested, and legally reviewed feature says otherwise, and must not give a caller the impression that help has been dispatched when it has not.
- Businesses that receive urgent but non emergency calls, for example a plumbing business receiving a flooding call, must configure escalation and callback time frames that match how fast they can actually respond, and must not advertise a response time they cannot meet.

## No medical advice

- ApexVoice does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, triage, treatment recommendations, medication information beyond factual information the business has provided for administrative purposes, or an assessment of urgency of a health condition.
- For dental and medical office customers, the receptionist is limited to administrative tasks: hours, location, appointment booking and changes, insurance and payment process information the office has provided, directions, and taking messages for clinical staff.
- If a caller describes symptoms or a health emergency, the receptionist must not assess the situation. It should direct an emergency to 911, and otherwise take a message or offer an appointment according to the office rules, and advise the caller to contact the office clinical staff, a pharmacist, a health information line in their province, or another appropriate professional as the office has configured.
- Callers are told not to share detailed medical information on the call. If a caller does share it, the information is treated as personal information under the Privacy Policy, and the business must handle it under its own health privacy duties, which may include PHIPA in Ontario.

## No legal advice, and no other regulated advice

- ApexVoice does not provide legal advice, does not assess the merits of a legal matter, does not calculate limitation periods or deadlines, and does not draft or file legal documents.
- For law firms and other professional service customers, the receptionist is limited to administrative tasks: hours, location, booking an initial consultation according to firm rules, taking a message, and providing general information the firm has provided about its process, including a statement that the firm must check for conflicts before accepting a matter, where the firm has configured that statement.
- The same principle applies to financial, accounting, insurance, real estate, and other regulated activities: the receptionist may provide factual business information the customer has provided and may book appointments and take messages, but it must not recommend a product, assess suitability, quote a binding price or rate, interpret a contract or a policy, or hold itself out as licensed.
- A price, fee, or rate the receptionist mentions is information the business provided, is not a binding quote unless the business has expressly configured it as one and applicable law allows it, and may change. Sales and onboarding material must not suggest otherwise.

## AI can make mistakes

- The receptionist uses AI models to understand speech, generate responses, and summarize calls. It can misunderstand accents, names, numbers, dates, and uncommon words. It can produce a summary that omits something important or states something the caller did not say. It can answer a question incorrectly if the business knowledge is incomplete, out of date, or ambiguous.
- Transcription can contain errors, especially for names, addresses, phone numbers, and technical terms. A transcript is an aid, not a certified record.
- Businesses must review messages, summaries, and bookings before acting on them where an error would matter, must verify phone numbers and appointment details with the caller where practical, and must correct errors in their configuration and knowledge when they find them.
- Harbour & Main does not guarantee that any specific call will be understood correctly, that any specific message is complete, or that AI output is fit for a regulated, clinical, legal, or safety decision. Do not use ApexVoice output as the sole basis for such a decision.

## No guaranteed bookings, messages, or notifications until a connected system confirms

- An appointment is booked only when the connected calendar or booking system confirms it. If the connection is unavailable, the slot is taken, or the system does not confirm, the receptionist should take a message or offer alternatives, and the dashboard must show the item as unconfirmed or pending, not booked.
- A message is delivered only when the delivery channel, email, SMS, dashboard, or integrated system, reports delivery or is shown in the dashboard as the system of record, as applicable to the configuration. Network, provider, spam filtering, and incorrect contact details can all prevent or delay delivery.
- Notifications to the business, for example a new message alert, depend on third party providers and the business own devices and settings. Harbour & Main does not guarantee instant or infallible notification.
- Businesses must check the dashboard on a schedule appropriate to their business, and must not rely only on a push or email alert for time sensitive matters.
- Demonstrations and sales material must show confirmation states honestly. Do not show a sample booking as confirmed unless the demonstration explains that, in a live account, confirmation comes from the connected booking system.

## Availability and accuracy limits

- The service depends on telephone networks, internet connectivity, and third party AI, speech, and hosting providers. Outages, provider changes, and call quality problems can affect whether a call is answered, understood, or recorded in the dashboard.
- The service answers calls to the configured numbers. It does not guarantee that a carrier forwarding rule, a business phone system setting, or a caller network will route every call correctly. Businesses should test forwarding and call flows after any change to their phone system.
- Language support is limited to the languages the live product actually supports and has been tested in. Do not advertise a language, dialect, voice, or accessibility feature that has not been tested for the customer configuration.
- Voice quality, caller background noise, speakerphone use, and poor connections reduce accuracy. The receptionist may ask a caller to repeat information, and may take a message instead of completing a task it cannot complete reliably.

## Business remains responsible for its customers

- ApexVoice is a tool for the business. The business remains responsible for its services, its advice, its prices, its appointment decisions, its follow up, and its relationship with its customers and callers.
- Using ApexVoice does not transfer a business professional, regulatory, or contractual duty to Harbour & Main.
- If a caller is dissatisfied, asks for a human, or raises a complaint, the business must have a human process to handle it. ApexVoice escalation and message taking support that process, they do not replace it.

## What ApexVoice is not

For clarity, ApexVoice is not:

- A human receptionist service, and it must not be sold or described as one.
- An answering service for emergencies, crisis support, or alarm monitoring.
- A medical, dental, legal, financial, or other professional adviser.
- A certified transcription service.
- A guarantee of more bookings, more revenue, or any business result. No sales material may state a result, saving, or statistic unless it is based on measured data for a stated period and customer group, reviewed before publication.
- A substitute for a business own privacy, security, or regulatory compliance program.

## Acknowledgement

During onboarding, the business owner or authorized manager should acknowledge these limitations, confirm an escalation path and emergency wording for their configuration, and confirm any regulated industry restrictions that apply to their template, before the receptionist takes live calls. That acknowledgement should be recorded with the account.
